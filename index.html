💡 General Q&A Prep — Questions any contact may ask you

❓ "What is seazn?"

Lead with the problem: women's health has been underfunded and underresearched for decades. Then: "seazn is [your 1-line product description]. We're building the [intelligence/companion/platform] that finally puts women in the centre of their own health data." Keep it to 2–3 sentences max — let them ask for more.

❓ "What's your traction?"

Lead with your strongest number first (users, MRR, retention, engagement — whatever is most impressive). Frame it with momentum: "We've gone from X to Y in Z months." If numbers are early, emphasise qualitative signal: waitlist, NPS, user quotes.

❓ "What's your business model?"

Be clear and direct: D2C subscription, B2B2C (employers/payers), or hybrid. State your current model AND where you see it evolving. Investors respect founders who understand their monetisation path, even if it's early.

❓ "What makes you different from [Hormona / Clue / other]?"

Acknowledge the category is growing (good for everyone) then pivot to your differentiation. Focus on what your approach uniquely enables — data model, clinical depth, user experience, specific population served. Don't be dismissive of competitors.

❓ "Why are you the right person to build this?"

Your founder-market fit story is powerful: you were a user first (Hormona), you felt the gap personally, and that drove you to build seazn. Then layer in your professional background. Investors bet on founders as much as products — let the personal story land.

❓ "What are you raising / what's the ask?"

State the round size and stage clearly. Follow with: "We're looking for [type of investor — strategic / lead / follow] and specifically want partners who bring [X — clinical network / payer relationships / distribution]." Tailor the 'what we want' to whoever you're talking to.

💡 Your Key Takeaways — "Venture Capitalists: Friend or Foe?" (Wed 13 May · 1:35 PM)

💼 Partner, Not Just Money

Think carefully about whether you want an investor helping make decisions — they don't need a board seat, but you can treat your lead investor like a board member: quarterly meetings, ask for their opinion, be transparent. Choose the right partner, not just the highest cheque.

👁 Board Observer Option

A board observer (non-voting) is a smart early-stage compromise — gives investors visibility and makes them feel like partners without giving away governance. Consider this over a full board seat at pre-seed.

🧭 Advisory vs Corporate Board

At early stage: lean on an advisory board over a corporate board. Advisors bring domain expertise and open doors without the governance complexity. Structure corporate board carefully and only when needed.

🚀 Show the Series A Path

Even at pre-seed: show investors you're already thinking about Series A and how you'll get there. What milestones will you hit with this round? What does the company look like at Series A? Investors want to see you think ahead.

🫀 Pre-seed = Story Over Numbers

Pre-seed investors invest in you as a person far more than your company. Lead with why you're the right founder to solve this problem. Make it personal, compelling, and specific. Numbers matter less than conviction and founder-market fit.

🤖 Leverage AI to De-Risk

Show investors you're using AI to de-risk the business — faster product iteration, better personalisation, lower cost to serve. In 2026, not mentioning AI is a red flag. Make it central to how you describe the product and defensibility.

📈 Momentum is the Metric

Investors invest in momentum. Come with numbers that show direction — even if small. Month-over-month user growth, engagement depth, interview volume, social following growth. Show the trajectory, not just the snapshot.
